03 / Connecticut

Six things
Connecticut
does differently.

Statements of Connecticut law, not claims about outcomes. Statutory references are drawn from the content plan and are confirmed by the attorney at drafting.

01

All-property state

A Connecticut court may assign any asset either spouse owns — including premarital, inherited and gifted property. C.G.S. §46b-81.

02

No alimony formula

There is no guideline calculation. The court weighs the statutory factors. C.G.S. §46b-82.

03

College support

An educational support order is available, but generally must be sought before the child turns eighteen. C.G.S. §46b-56c.

04

The nonadversarial track

A streamlined dissolution for short marriages with no children and limited assets, with strict eligibility. C.G.S. §§46b-44a–44c.

05

Coercive control

Since Jennifer's Law, a pattern of controlling behavior can support a civil restraining order without physical violence. P.A. 21-78.

06

No common-law marriage

Living together does not create a marriage in Connecticut, however long the relationship.

All 38 Questions →
Is Connecticut a 50/50 divorce state?+

No. Property is divided equitably, and the court may assign any asset either spouse owns.

Can the court divide property I owned before the marriage?+

Yes. Connecticut is an all-property state, so premarital, inherited and gifted assets can be reached.

How long does a divorce take in Connecticut?+

There is a statutory waiting period after the return date, and contested cases run substantially longer.

Do I need a reason to get divorced in Connecticut?+

No. Irretrievable breakdown is enough. Fault grounds exist but are not required.

What is a nonadversarial divorce?+

A streamlined Connecticut track for short marriages with no children and limited assets, subject to strict eligibility.

What are the automatic orders?+

Court orders binding both spouses from the start of the case, restricting transfers, travel with children and insurance changes.

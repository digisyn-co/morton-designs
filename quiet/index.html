03  —  Approach

How a Connecticut case runs.

01Consultation

Your position, the assets, the children, and the range of realistic outcomes. A conflict check runs first.

02Automatic orders

P.B. §25-5 binds both parties from the start of the case, restricting transfers, travel with children and insurance changes.

03Financial affidavit

Connecticut runs on the sworn affidavit. Incomplete disclosure is the most common cause of delay and cost.

04Family Services

Mediation and, where ordered, a family relations study. Most matters resolve here.

05Judgment

Where agreement is not possible, the case is heard at 90 Washington Street in Hartford.

04  —  Connecticut

Six things Connecticut does differently.

Statements of Connecticut law, not claims about outcomes. Statutory references are drawn from the content plan and are confirmed by the attorney at drafting.

01

All-property state

A Connecticut court may assign any asset either spouse owns — including premarital, inherited and gifted property. C.G.S. §46b-81.

02

No alimony formula

There is no guideline calculation. The court weighs the statutory factors. C.G.S. §46b-82.

03

College support

An educational support order is available, but generally must be sought before the child turns eighteen. C.G.S. §46b-56c.

04

The nonadversarial track

A streamlined dissolution for short marriages with no children and limited assets. C.G.S. §§46b-44a–44c.

05

Coercive control

Since Jennifer's Law, a pattern of controlling behavior can support a civil restraining order without physical violence. P.A. 21-78.

06

No common-law marriage

Living together does not create a marriage in Connecticut, however long the relationship.

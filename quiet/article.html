Most people arrive at a family law matter having already made the hardest decision. What follows is procedure, and procedure rewards preparation.

01The first conference

The first conference is not a formality. It establishes the facts, the asset position, and the arrangements for any children, and it is where the range of realistic outcomes is set out for the first time. Expect it to run an hour or more, and expect to be asked questions that feel intrusive. They are the questions the other side will ask later.

You should leave with three things: a written summary of your position, an outline of the next steps, and a costs agreement. If any of those are missing, ask for them.

02What you will be asked for

Financial disclosure is an obligation, not a negotiating position. Both parties must give a full and frank account of their financial circumstances, and the obligation continues for the life of the matter. Incomplete disclosure is the single most common cause of delay and cost.

Disclosure is not something you survive. It is the ground the whole matter is argued on.

03Decisions that cannot wait

A small number of decisions are time-sensitive: whether to separate under one roof, how joint accounts and redraw facilities are handled, and what interim arrangements apply to the children. Each can affect the final outcome, and each is easier to address before positions harden.

Where there is a risk to assets or to safety, urgent applications are available and should be considered immediately rather than held in reserve.

04What happens next

Once disclosure is exchanged and the pool is established, most matters move to negotiation or mediation. A minority proceed to litigation. Either way the preparation is the same, which is why it is done properly at the start rather than in response to a court date.

The measure of a well-run matter is not how hard it was fought. It is whether you understood every decision at the time you made it.

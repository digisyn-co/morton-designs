Divorce

7 questions
Is Connecticut a 50/50 divorce state?+

No. Property is divided equitably, and the court may assign any asset either spouse owns.

Can the court divide property I owned before the marriage?+

Yes. Connecticut is an all-property state, so premarital, inherited and gifted assets can be reached.

How long does a divorce take in Connecticut?+

There is a statutory waiting period after the return date, and contested cases run substantially longer.

Do I need a reason to get divorced in Connecticut?+

No. Irretrievable breakdown is enough. Fault grounds exist but are not required.

What is a nonadversarial divorce?+

A streamlined Connecticut track for short marriages with no children and limited assets, subject to strict eligibility.

What are the automatic orders?+

Court orders binding both spouses from the start of the case, restricting transfers, travel with children and insurance changes.

How is alimony calculated in Connecticut?+

There is no formula. The court weighs statutory factors including length of marriage, earnings and needs.

Custody & Visitation

6 questions
Does joint custody mean equal time in Connecticut?+

No. Joint legal custody concerns decision-making; the parenting plan sets the schedule.

What goes into a parenting plan?+

Decision-making, the residential schedule, holidays, communication and how disputes get resolved.

At what age can my child decide where to live?+

There is no magic age. A child's informed preference is weighed according to age and maturity.

Can I move out of state with my child?+

Not unilaterally after judgment. Relocation requires agreement or a court order under the statutory standard.

Do grandparents have visitation rights in Connecticut?+

Only on a demanding showing that includes a parent-like relationship and real and significant harm without visitation.

What does a guardian ad litem do?+

Investigates and reports on the child's best interests. The court allocates the cost between the parents.

Child Support

5 questions
How is child support calculated in Connecticut?+

The guidelines use an income-shares model based on both parents' combined net income.

Who pays for college after a divorce?+

The court can enter an educational support order, but it generally must be sought before the child turns eighteen.

What counts as income for child support?+

Wages, bonuses, self-employment income and many benefits, less specified allowable deductions.

When does child support end in Connecticut?+

Generally at eighteen, or later if the child is still in high school, with exceptions.

Can the court order support that differs from the guidelines?+

Yes, on stated deviation criteria such as shared custody or extraordinary expenses.

Modification

4 questions
What is a substantial change in circumstances?+

A meaningful change since the last order. It is the threshold for most modifications.

Does cohabitation end alimony in Connecticut?+

Not automatically. The court may modify or terminate if living with another person alters financial needs.

Does support drop automatically if I lose my job?+

No. The order stands until the court modifies it, so filing promptly protects you.

Can alimony be modified after the judgment?+

Only if the judgment did not make it non-modifiable, and on a substantial change.

Enforcement & Contempt

4 questions
What can I do if my ex stops paying support?+

File a motion for contempt. Remedies include arrearage findings, income withholding and attorney's fees.

Can someone be jailed for contempt in family court?+

Confinement is possible for wilful non-compliance, subject to defenses and purge conditions.

What if my ex won't follow the parenting plan?+

Access orders are enforceable by the same contempt process as financial orders.

I was served with a contempt motion — what now?+

Wilfulness must be proven and inability to comply is a defense, but the deadlines are short.

Prenuptial & Postnuptial

4 questions
Are prenuptial agreements enforceable in Connecticut?+

Yes, under the Connecticut Premarital Agreement Act, if properly made and disclosed.

Can we sign an agreement after the wedding?+

Yes. Connecticut recognizes postnuptial agreements, judged on a stricter fair-and-equitable standard.

Can a prenup be set aside?+

On limited grounds, chiefly involuntariness, unconscionability, or inadequate financial disclosure.

Does Connecticut recognize common-law marriage?+

No. Living together does not create a marriage, whatever the length of the relationship.

Domestic Violence

5 questions
What happens after a family violence arrest in Connecticut?+

Arraignment comes very quickly, and protective-order conditions are typically set at that first appearance.

What is the Family Violence Education Program?+

A diversionary program that can lead to dismissal for eligible cases.

What is the difference between a protective order and a restraining order?+

One issues from the criminal case. The other is a civil order applied for by the protected party.

What is coercive control under Jennifer's Law?+

A pattern of controlling behavior that can support a civil restraining order without physical violence.

How does a family violence case affect custody?+

It bears directly on the best-interest analysis and can shape or restrict the parenting schedule.
